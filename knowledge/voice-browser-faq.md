# What the voice browser can and cannot do

- It can open any public website, search the web, click links and buttons, type into search boxes and forms, scroll, read pages aloud, list the options on a page and show them on the caller's screen, compare options, add items to carts, and complete checkouts after a spoken confirmation.
- It cannot type passwords, card numbers or one-time codes, solve captchas, or sign in on the caller's behalf: the caller does those in the browser window on their screen, once per site, and the session is kept.
- It cannot see the page unless it looks: every action returns the fresh page state, and a screenshot goes with each browsing request.
- It does not place an order, send, post or delete anything without reading back exactly what will happen and hearing a clear yes.
- Two voices, one person: a speech-to-speech model handles the greeting and chat; the browsing pipeline takes over for anything on the web and hands back when the task is done. Both use the same voice family so the swap is not noticeable.
- Typical timing: opening a page two to five seconds; a search three to six seconds; a click one to three seconds. The assistant says what it is doing while it works.
